% ── Abstract ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{abstract}
This paper presents a systematic comparative evaluation of news headline
classification across 24 experimental configurations. We evaluate three
preprocessing strategies (none, extreme, and optimum), two word representations
(TF-IDF and Skip-gram Word2Vec), and eight model architectures---Logistic
Regression, Deep Neural Network (DNN), SimpleRNN, GRU, LSTM, BiSimpleRNN,
BiGRU, and BiLSTM---on a 4-class dataset of 88,829 training headlines. All
models are evaluated on a held-out test set of 12,000 samples (3,000 per class)
using accuracy, macro-F1, confusion matrices, and per-class classification
reports. The best-performing configuration is \textbf{TF-IDF + DeepNN with
extreme preprocessing}, achieving 91.16\% accuracy and macro-F1 of 0.9111. The
worst is \textbf{Skip-gram + BiSimpleRNN with no preprocessing}, yielding only
33.66\% accuracy and macro-F1 of 0.2552 due to near-complete class collapse.
SimpleRNN consistently fails under no and optimum preprocessing owing to
vanishing gradient instability, while gated architectures (GRU, LSTM, BiGRU,
BiLSTM) remain robust across all settings.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
news headline classification, TF-IDF, Word2Vec, Skip-gram, LSTM, GRU,
bidirectional RNN, text preprocessing, NLP, deep learning
\end{IEEEkeywords}

% ── I. Introduction ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Introduction}

The automatic categorisation of news headlines into topic classes is a
foundational Natural Language Processing (NLP) task with applications in news
aggregation, recommendation systems, and misinformation detection. Unlike
full-article classification, headlines are characteristically short
($\approx$8 words on average), syntactically compressed, and context-sparse,
making preprocessing and representation choices especially consequential.

This project investigates how different preprocessing pipelines and word
representations interact with model architecture choices to influence
classification performance. We compare classical TF-IDF vectorisation against
neural Skip-gram (Word2Vec) embeddings, pairing each with models ranging from
Logistic Regression to Bidirectional LSTMs. A structured grid search selects
hyperparameters for each configuration, ensuring fair comparison across all 24
experimental settings.

The dataset comprises 88,829 labelled training headlines across four categories:
\textit{Business}, \textit{Science and Technology}, \textit{Sports}, and
\textit{World News}. The training distribution is significantly imbalanced---Sports
(40.2\%) and World News (35.6\%) dominate, while Business (13.3\%) and
Science and Technology (11.0\%) are underrepresented---which impacts per-class
performance, particularly for minority classes.

The primary contributions of this work are: (i) a rigorous 24-configuration
grid experiment with consistent evaluation methodology; (ii) identification of
critical failure modes for SimpleRNN and BiSimpleRNN under certain preprocessing
strategies; and (iii) practical recommendations for fast yet accurate headline
classifiers applicable to real-world deployment.

EDA revealed that stopwords (\textit{the, to, a, in, of}) heavily dominate raw

raw headlines confirms this pattern. Word counts follow a right-skewed
distribution (mode $\approx$7 words, max $\approx$45 words after HTML
stripping).

\subsection{Preprocessing Strategies}

Three preprocessing variants were designed to isolate the effect of text
normalisation:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{None:} Raw headlines passed as-is. Retains HTML artefacts
    (e.g., \texttt{\&\#39;s} appears at rank 12 in top-30 token frequencies),
    capitalisation, and punctuation. Provides the cleaning-free baseline.

    \item \textbf{Extreme:} Full pipeline applied in sequence: HTML unescaping
    $\to$ tag stripping $\to$ URL removal $\to$ non-alphabetic removal $\to$
    lowercasing $\to$ tokenisation $\to$ all-stopword removal $\to$ WordNet
    lemmatisation $\to$ Porter stemming. Produces the most compact vocabulary
    but destroys negation cues.

    \item \textbf{Optimum:} Identical to extreme but with two modifications:
    (i) stemming is \textit{omitted}---stemming over-conflates morphological
    variants in short text; and (ii) negation words (\textit{no, not, nor}) are
    \textit{preserved} by excluding them from the stopword filter. Negation
    can flip a headline's topic boundary (e.g., \textit{``not guilty''} $\to$
    Politics/World News vs.\ Crime).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Word Representations}

\textbf{TF-IDF:} Term Frequency--Inverse Document Frequency vectorisation
transforms cleaned text into sparse, high-dimensional vectors. Grid-searched
parameters: \texttt{max\_features} $\in$ \{5\,000, 8\,000\},
\texttt{ngram\_range} $\in$ \{(1,1), (1,2)\}, \texttt{min\_df}=2. Best
configurations used \texttt{max\_features}=8,000 and unigrams across all three
preprocessing variants. Bigrams underperformed on all configurations.

\textbf{Skip-gram Word2Vec:} A shallow neural network trained on training
sentences per preprocessing variant with: \texttt{vector\_size}=100,
\texttt{window}=5, \texttt{min\_count}=2, \texttt{sg}=1 (Skip-gram),
\texttt{epochs}=10, \texttt{seed}=42. The embedding matrix was frozen during
sequence model training (\texttt{trainable=False}) to reduce parameters and
prevent overfitting given the modest corpus size ($\approx$75k short
sentences).

\subsection{Model Architectures}

Eight models spanning three families were evaluated:

\subsubsection{TF-IDF Models}

\textbf{Logistic Regression (LR):} L2-regularised multinomial LR. Grid:
$C \in \{0.5, 1.0, 2.0\}$, \texttt{solver}=lbfgs, \texttt{max\_iter}=400.
Best $C=2.0$ across all preprocessing variants.

\textbf{Deep Neural Network (DNN):} Three-layer feedforward network:
\texttt{Dense(dense1, ReLU)} $\to$ \texttt{Dropout} $\to$
\texttt{Dense(dense2, ReLU)} $\to$ \texttt{Dropout} $\to$
\texttt{Dense(64, ReLU)} $\to$ \texttt{Dense(4, Softmax)}. Grid:
\texttt{dense1} $\in$ \{256, 384\}, \texttt{dense2} $\in$ \{128, 192\},
\texttt{dropout} $\in$ \{0.35, 0.5\}, \texttt{lr}=$10^{-3}$,
\texttt{batch\_size}=64, \texttt{epochs}=8, EarlyStopping patience=2.

\subsubsection{Skip-gram Sequence Models}

All six sequence models share the architecture:
\texttt{Embedding(frozen W2V, dim=100)} $\to$ [RNN variant] $\to$
\texttt{Dropout} $\to$ \texttt{Dense(4, Softmax)}. Sequences are
padded/truncated to \texttt{max\_len}=60 (covering the 97th percentile of
training headline lengths). Grid: \texttt{units} $\in$ \{64, 96\},
\texttt{dropout} $\in$ \{0.4, 0.5\}, \texttt{lr}=$10^{-3}$,
\texttt{batch\_size}=64, \texttt{epochs}=6, EarlyStopping patience=2.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{SimpleRNN:} Vanilla recurrent unit; fast but susceptible to
    vanishing gradients.
    \item \textbf{GRU:} Gated Recurrent Unit; fewer parameters than LSTM with
    competitive performance.
    \item \textbf{LSTM:} Long Short-Term Memory; explicit memory cell handles
    longer-range dependencies.
    \item \textbf{BiSimpleRNN / BiGRU / BiLSTM:} Bidirectional wrappers
    processing sequences in both forward and backward directions, doubling
    effective hidden state and capturing full left-right context.
\end{itemize}

% ── IV. Discussion ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Best Configuration: extreme + TF-IDF + DNN}

Accuracy: \textbf{91.16\%} | Macro F1: \textbf{0.9111}. Extreme preprocessing
reduces vocabulary noise (stopwords, HTML artefacts visible as \texttt{\#39;s}
at rank 12 in raw token frequencies). The compact, high-quality TF-IDF vectors
are then fully exploited by the DNN. EarlyStopping triggered at epoch 4 with
\texttt{dense1}=256, \texttt{dense2}=128, \texttt{dropout}=0.35, confirming
rapid convergence. Sports achieves the highest recall (0.98) due to distinctive
sports vocabulary. Business has the lowest recall (0.85), confused with World
News for economy-related headlines.

\subsection{Worst Configuration: none + Skip-gram + BiSimpleRNN}

Accuracy: \textbf{33.66\%} | Macro F1: \textbf{0.2552}. This configuration
completely fails---the model predicts nearly all samples as \textit{Science and
Technology} (precision 0.27, recall 0.99) while assigning near-zero recall to
all other classes. No preprocessing leaves HTML noise (\texttt{<html>},
\texttt{<b>}, entity codes) and inconsistent tokenisation in the Word2Vec
corpus, producing degenerate embeddings. BiSimpleRNN then collapses to a
majority-of-corpus class prediction, yielding a precision of 1.00 and recall
of 0.00 for Business.

\subsection{Preprocessing Effect Analysis}

A nuanced pattern emerges. Extreme preprocessing yields the single \textit{best}
result when paired with TF-IDF but produces the worst Skip-gram results among
gated models. The mechanism differs by representation:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{TF-IDF + extreme:} Extreme preprocessing concentrates
    TF-IDF weight on discriminative topic tokens (e.g., \textit{win, goal,
    earn, govern}) after stopword removal. Best val F1 = 0.9199 (DNN,
    \texttt{dense1}=256, \texttt{dense2}=128, \texttt{dropout}=0.35).

    \item \textbf{Skip-gram + extreme:} Aggressive stemming conflates
    morphological variants, weakening embedding quality for named entities
    (player names, company names). SimpleRNN especially struggles, collapsing
    on all three preprocessing variants.

    \item \textbf{None preprocessing:} Raw HTML tokens corrupt Skip-gram
    embeddings. The token \texttt{\#39;s} appearing at rank 12 (23,970
    occurrences) consumes embedding capacity that should represent meaningful
    words, causing catastrophic class collapse in BiSimpleRNN.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{SimpleRNN Failure Analysis}

SimpleRNN is the most sensitive architecture: macro-F1 of 0.34, 0.52, and 0.60
under none, optimum, and extreme preprocessing respectively. Training logs show
oscillating validation accuracy (e.g., epoch 1: 0.47 $\to$ epoch 2: 0.42 $\to$
epoch 3: 0.40 under none preprocessing), indicating vanishing gradient
collapse. The short \texttt{max\_len}=60 limit constrains the gradient signal,
and the absent gating mechanism cannot resolve conflicting dependencies. By
contrast, GRU and LSTM---which differ only in their gating---consistently
achieve macro-F1 $\geq$ 0.87 across all settings.

\subsection{TF-IDF vs.\ Skip-gram Representations}

For stable models (LR, DNN, GRU, LSTM, BiGRU, BiLSTM), TF-IDF and Skip-gram
produce comparable results ($\Delta$ macro-F1 $\leq$ 0.015). TF-IDF converges
faster and requires no pre-training corpus. Skip-gram's advantage emerges only
when embeddings are high quality (extreme preprocessing) and gated models are
used. The practical implication: \textbf{TF-IDF with DNN is the preferred
production baseline} for this dataset, offering the best accuracy at the
lowest computational cost.

\subsection{Hyperparameter Decision Rationale}

Key hyperparameter choices and their justification:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{TF-IDF max\_features=8,000:} Larger vocabulary captures more
    domain-specific terms without excessive memory overhead. Bigrams
    underperformed---short headlines rarely contain fixed-phrase bigrams that
    TF-IDF weights consistently across classes.

    \item \textbf{LR $C$=2.0:} Weaker regularisation allows the model to
    fit the relatively clean TF-IDF space. All three preprocessing variants
    selected $C$=2.0 as best.

    \item \textbf{DNN dropout=0.35:} Moderate dropout prevents memorisation
    of TF-IDF feature indices specific to the training set while retaining
    sufficient capacity in the dense layers.

    \item \textbf{Word2Vec window=5:} Captures local context typical of short
    headline constructs; larger windows conflate topic-unrelated
    co-occurrences in the compact training corpus.

    \item \textbf{max\_len=60:} Covers the 97th percentile of training
    headline lengths; truncation affects fewer than 3\% of samples.

    \item \textbf{EarlyStopping patience=2:} Prevents overfitting on the
    validation set while converging within 2--6 epochs for most
    configurations, as observed in training logs.

    \item \textbf{RNN units=64:} Best performance for most gated models.
    units=96 sometimes underperformed (e.g., GRU none: val F1 0.8394 vs.\
    0.8953 for units=64), suggesting over-parameterisation relative to the
    modest corpus size.
\end{itemize}

% ── V. Conclusion ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Conclusion}

This study conducted 24 controlled experiments on news headline classification,
yielding four actionable findings:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Extreme preprocessing + TF-IDF + DNN} is the best
    configuration (acc=91.16\%, F1=0.9111). Aggressive cleaning benefits
    bag-of-words models by concentrating TF-IDF weight on discriminative
    topic tokens, contrary to common intuition that preserving text is
    always beneficial.

    \item \textbf{SimpleRNN and BiSimpleRNN are unreliable} for this task:
    4 of 6 configurations produce near-random or collapsed predictions.
    Gated units (GRU/LSTM) are strongly preferred for any production
    deployment on short-text classification tasks.

    \item \textbf{No-preprocessing with Skip-gram} is the highest-risk
    combination---raw HTML tokens corrupt embeddings and cause catastrophic
    class collapse in architectures lacking gating mechanisms.

    \item \textbf{Logistic Regression remains competitive}: with extreme
    preprocessing it scores F1=0.8975, close to BiLSTM+Skip-gram (0.9082),
    while training in seconds versus hours.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Limitations:} Skip-gram is trained on $\approx$75k short
headlines; richer pre-trained embeddings (GloVe, fastText) may close the
TF-IDF gap. Embeddings are frozen---joint fine-tuning could benefit BiLSTM.
No transformer models (BERT, DistilBERT) were evaluated due to compute
constraints. The 4-class imbalance (Sports 40\% vs.\ Sci\&Tech 11\%) skews
per-class metrics toward majority classes.

\noindent\textbf{Future Work:} Fine-tuning DistilBERT on the same dataset
would likely exceed 95\% accuracy. Data augmentation (back-translation,
synonym substitution) could reduce the imbalance penalty for Business and
Science \& Technology. A production API wrapping the best model
(TF-IDF + DNN) could be deployed with sub-10ms inference latency.
